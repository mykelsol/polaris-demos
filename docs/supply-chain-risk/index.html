Summary Generated when this pattern formed · updated 2h ago
One corridor, four projects, both cable chains — escalating.
What changed

Nine signals in three weeks point one way. Hormuz transits fell from 129 a day to 4 by 10 March. Jebel Ali discharge fell from 3,832 containers to 11 by 14 March. War-risk premiums went from 0.25% of hull value to between 1% and 7.5%.

Why this became a pattern

These signals were grouped because they share a corridor, a time window and a direction. Two monitored thresholds were crossed: insurance within 48 hours of 28 February, and port congestion by 6 March. Neither was a judgement call — the thresholds were defined in advance.

What it touches

Four of nine projects: Al Nasr P5, Marjan, Nasr-115, North Field. Power, control and instrumentation cable plus power distribution. Both copper chains and both electrical chains route through the same corridor, so supplier choice does not reduce the exposure.

What's needed

A decision is in progress with the Electrical category manager. Logistics has opened a second case on the same corridor. Nothing further is needed from this screen.

What is happening
Situation

Iran restricted commercial transits through the Strait of Hormuz on 28 February following a naval incident. Transit volumes have fallen for three consecutive weeks and war-risk premiums have risen 40% over the same period, with no published timeline for reopening.

Exposure

Four of the six Gulf projects source cable and electrical components through the corridor. Window to make a re-route decision closes in 12 days; missing it delays site energization by an estimated 5–7 weeks per project.

● 7 affected suppliers · 2 shared dependencies
Watch indicators

Reassessing if transit volumes recover for two consecutive weeks, if war-risk premiums fall below 20% over baseline, or if any of the four suppliers reports a confirmed delivery slip past 27 March.

Reroute affected shipments via Cape diversionChosen · Mar 15

Cape routing adds 9–11 days versus the corridor but avoids the restriction entirely and holds up regardless of whether the corridor reopens, recovers partially, or stays closed through Q2. Charter capacity on the diversion route is confirmed available for the next three sailings.

Does this hold up if the corridor reopens early?

Yes. The Cape diversion is chosen because it holds up in every scenario, including early reopening — the recovery-plan request continues in parallel and can be stood down without cost if the corridor clears.
Why not the alternatives

Inventory pull-forward cannot be sized without McDermott's inventory and required-on-site data. Continued monitoring does not meet the objective; the corridor crossed its red threshold on 10 March.
Confidence and limits

Corridor findings are sourced and real but carry a corrected, pending re-verification badge. Schedule impact in days is not available without McDermott's schedule and PO data.
